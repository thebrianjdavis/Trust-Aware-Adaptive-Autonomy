# About

## About the Research

Trust-Aware Adaptive Autonomy is a research software project for studying
reliance-aware adaptation in human–machine resource-allocation teams. It uses
controlled simulation, interpretable models, and reproducible experiments to
separate two related questions: whether adaptive recommendation strength can
regulate simulated reliance behavior and whether that regulation improves task
performance. The repository remains a research prototype, and its
simulation-based evidence should not be interpreted as human-subject
validation. See [Research Impact](research/impact.md) for the intended
scientific contribution and current evidentiary limits.

## Previous Research

Brian’s master’s thesis,
[*Leveraging Counterfactuals for Enhanced Natural Language Inference: A Multitask Knowledge Distillation Approach*](https://rave.ohiolink.edu/etdc/view?acc_num=wright1748367632205921),
investigated whether counterfactual examples could improve natural language
inference through multitask knowledge distillation. That work explored
counterfactual reasoning, model behavior, and methods for transferring
knowledge into smaller language models.

His current research extends this broader interest in trustworthy and
understandable artificial intelligence into human–AI teaming, where the central
question is not only whether an AI system can produce a useful recommendation,
but also when and how a human teammate should rely on it. The project’s
[research questions](overview/research-questions.md) frame that inquiry without
implying that the two research efforts use the same methodology.
